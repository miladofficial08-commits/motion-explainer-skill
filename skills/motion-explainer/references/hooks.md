# Hook and hold database (full)

52 patterns. Use with the 'Story first' step in SKILL.md. Machine-readable copy: `hooks.json` in this folder.

**Evidence:** `M` = gemessen: a primary source (study, platform statement, own measurement) supports the pattern or its mechanism, at the level named. `P` = praxis: craft or opinion. Strength is `mittel` at best; nothing here is proven to make a short perform.

## How to use for any niche

1. Fill four slots from your idea: **object** (one thing you can draw), **viewer situation** (one moment they know), **mismatch** (what they believe vs what is true), **payoff** (what you will show by beat 3).
2. Find the shape of the idea in the table below and take 3 candidate hooks from it.
3. Write each candidate as beat 1 (and 2). Max 6 words per sentence. Put sourced values only; brackets in examples are placeholders.

| Idea shape | Candidate hooks / holds |
|---|---|
| misconception (people believe X) | H02, H03, H28, M07 |
| mechanism (why does X happen) | H01, H10, H21, H29, H23 |
| tips / list | H05, H30, H25, H06 |
| comparison | H18, H02, H28 |
| story / experiment | H07, H17, H20, H09 |
| number / scale | H22, H21, H14, H13 |
| relatable pain | H10, H11, H12, H27 |
| ELI5 / analogy / dialogue | H23, H24, H01 |

## Read this first

- No controlled comparison of hook types on short video exists in the sources we found. 'gemessen' means a primary source (study, platform statement, own measurement) supports the pattern or its mechanism, at the level named in 'ebene'; it does not mean the pattern is proven to make a video perform.
- Platform timing advice conflicts: TikTok's own pages say hook within 6 s and proposition within 3 s; Shorts guides say 1-3 s; none gives an official retention benchmark.
- Vendor statistics (89 percent 1-second retention, 2.4x, 68 percent completion and similar) are attributed to reports we could not trace. They are not used as evidence.
- The Zeigarnik argument for open loops (unfinished tasks are remembered better) did not hold in a 2025 meta-analysis; the gap-of-curiosity argument (Loewenstein) and resumption (Ovsiankina) are what we rely on.
- Examples are wording demos. Any factual claim or number in a real video needs a source on screen (motion-explainer rule). Brackets mark where a sourced value goes.
- Meta and Instagram claims reach us through trade sites, not from the original posts.

## Hooks

### H01 Information gap on a familiar thing  [M, mittel]
- Formula: Ask or state something about [familiar object] that the viewer cannot yet explain.
- Why it works: A specific unanswered question about something the viewer half-knows creates a felt gap; people want to close it and remember the answer better when curious. Needs some prior knowledge.
- Tech: Why is your charger warm?
- Fitness: Why are you sore two days later?
- Finance: Where does your salary go first?
- Evidence level: labor-mechanismus (S4, S5)
- Careful: Lab studies with trivia questions, not videos. Curiosity is low if the viewer knows nothing about the topic.

### H02 Misconception first  [M, mittel]
- Formula: State what most people believe about [thing], then break it with [fact].
- Why it works: Confronting the wrong model forces effort; learners in one set of misconception-focused videos learned more than from clear, plain explanations.
- Tech: Deleting a file does not delete it.
- Fitness: Soreness is not proof the workout worked.
- Finance: A big salary does not make you rich.
- Evidence level: video-experiment (Autor-Zusammenfassung) (S9)
- Careful: Effect shown for learning, not for scroll retention. Peer-reviewed paper not verified by us. Only use a misconception the audience really holds.

### H03 Counterintuitive claim plus reason  [P, schwach]
- Formula: [Counterintuitive claim about thing] + promise of [specific reason].
- Why it works: A claim that clashes with expectation breaks autopilot and demands an explanation.
- Tech: More megapixels will not give a better photo.
- Fitness: Resting more can make you stronger.
- Finance: Paying the cheapest debt first can cost you.
- Evidence level: Vendor-Praxis (S21, S22)
- Careful: Vendor statistics (e.g. authority hooks get more comments) are not sourced. The claim must be true and sourceable.

### H04 Negative frame  [M, mittel]
- Formula: Stop [habit] / [thing] is quietly costing you [loss].
- Why it works: In 105,000 headline variants tested on real readers, more negative words meant more clicks; sad wording beat angry or scary wording.
- Tech: Stop reusing the same password.
- Fitness: Stop doing crunches for abs.
- Finance: Stop leaving cash in checking.
- Evidence level: Feldexperiment (Headlines, nicht Video) (S6)
- Careful: Headlines from the early social-media era, not short video. Do not exaggerate harm; never deflate the promise.

### H05 Numbered promise with time cap  [P, schwach]
- Formula: [N] [things] for [problem] in [T] seconds.
- Why it works: Names the value and caps the time cost; a bigger N makes the gap look bigger.
- Tech: 3 phone settings to change in 30 seconds.
- Fitness: 4 desk stretches in 40 seconds.
- Finance: 5 money rules in 45 seconds.
- Evidence level: interne Prinzipien + Vendor-Praxis (S21, S28)
- Careful: Not measured by us. Every number in a video still needs a source or must be a count of things in the video itself.

### H06 Plain statement of intent (message early)  [M, schwach]
- Formula: Say plainly what the viewer will see or learn: [what you will show].
- Why it works: Meta lists it as an official hook type; Meta reports that putting message and brand in the first 5 s made ads 1.7x more likely to rank top for purchase intent.
- Tech: Here is how Wi-Fi reaches your phone.
- Fitness: Here is a squat, joint by joint.
- Finance: Here is where one euro of tax goes.
- Evidence level: Plattform-Zahl (Meta, Anzeigen, aus zweiter Hand) (S2, S3)
- Careful: Claim concerns ads and purchase intent, relayed by a trade site. Weak for organic explainers.

### H07 Mid-action open  [P, schwach]
- Formula: Start at the peak moment of [event], explain after.
- Why it works: No setup means the first frame already holds tension; the viewer wants context.
- Tech: Screen goes black. Phone restarts itself.
- Fitness: Bar on your back. Legs start shaking.
- Finance: Card declined. Bill on the table.
- Evidence level: Vendor-Praxis (S21, S22)
- Careful: Vendor figure (31 percent higher completion) is unverified.

### H08 Incomplete visual  [P, schwach]
- Formula: Frame 1 shows [unusual thing] without explaining it; a caption names only what to watch.
- Why it works: An unexplained image is a question the eye asks before the ear hears anything.
- Tech: Frame 1: one chip glows inside an open phone.
- Fitness: Frame 1: one muscle fibre tears.
- Finance: Frame 1: one coin shrinks in a jar.
- Evidence level: Vendor-Praxis (S22)
- Careful: Vendor figure (2.4x vs text hooks) attributed to a report we could not verify.

### H09 Pattern interrupt  [P, schwach]
- Formula: Break the expected look or sound of [format] in the first second, then land the hook.
- Why it works: A sudden change triggers an automatic orienting response to new input (lab basis for cuts); overuse drains capacity.
- Tech: Glitch cut, then: this is not a bug.
- Fitness: Freeze-frame mid-jump: this is where it goes wrong.
- Finance: Sound cuts. One red number on black.
- Evidence level: Vendor-Praxis (Mechanismus: Lang, Labor) (S21, S22, S14)
- Careful: Vendor figure (89 percent 1-second retention) is unverified. Lang's work: related cuts help, unrelated cuts do not reach memory.

### H10 Self-identifying question  [P, schwach]
- Formula: Ever [exact situation the viewer has been in]?
- Why it works: The viewer recognises their own moment and holds the question themselves. Meta names question or invitation as an official hook type.
- Tech: Ever typed a word and your phone changed it?
- Fitness: Ever trained hard and seen no change?
- Finance: Ever checked your balance and wondered where it went?
- Evidence level: Plattform-Empfehlung ohne Zahl + Vendor-Praxis (S2, S21)
- Careful: Name the situation, not the person's job; naming a job excludes viewers (content-principles).

### H11 Direct address to a situation  [P, schwach]
- Formula: You [specific daily situation]. [Consequence].
- Why it works: A specific, recognisable situation feels sent to one person.
- Tech: You open 40 tabs. Here is the cost.
- Fitness: You sit all day. Your hips notice.
- Finance: You are paid on the 1st. By the 15th it is gone.
- Evidence level: Vendor-Praxis (S22)
- Careful: Vendor figure (2.1x saves) unverified. Keep the specificity in the object, not the audience label.

### H12 Stakes / loss framing  [P, schwach]
- Formula: [Thing] costs you [loss] if you ignore [detail].
- Why it works: Names what the viewer stands to lose, which raises the value of the next seconds.
- Tech: One weak password can empty an account.
- Fitness: Skip this warm-up and your shoulder pays.
- Finance: This fee quietly eats your savings.
- Evidence level: Vendor-Praxis (S22)
- Careful: Vendor figure (68 percent completion) unverified. The loss must be real; invented danger destroys trust.

### H13 Named-source lead  [P, schwach]
- Formula: [Named source] checked [N] [things]. One result stood out.
- Why it works: Credible, concrete provenance makes the claim worth the time.
- Tech: [Named lab] tested [N] phones. One result stood out.
- Fitness: [Named study], [N] lifters, one surprise.
- Finance: [Named report] checked [N] accounts. Look at this.
- Evidence level: Vendor-Praxis (S22, S24)
- Careful: Brackets are placeholders: use only a source you have actually read; then show it on screen.

### H14 Specific number or object instead of category  [P, schwach]
- Formula: A [named object / number / date] did [surprising thing].
- Why it works: Specific words can be pictured; category words trigger nothing.
- Tech: A [price] cable can ruin a [price] laptop.
- Fitness: A [number]-minute habit changed my [lift].
- Finance: A [number]-euro fee cost [number] euros.
- Evidence level: interne Prinzipien + Buchpraxis (S28, S24)
- Careful: Never invent the numbers; placeholders show where a sourced value goes.

### H15 Surprising fact reveal  [P, schwach]
- Formula: Did you know [familiar thing] has/does [unexpected fact]?
- Why it works: A small surprise promises that the video holds more of them.
- Tech: Did you know your phone has a barometer?
- Fitness: Did you know your heart slows when you exhale?
- Finance: Did you know bank holidays delay payments?
- Evidence level: Creator-Praxis (S20)
- Careful: vidIQ's example view counts are anecdotes, not evidence for the pattern.

### H16 Hidden information  [P, schwach]
- Formula: The [thing] that [institution] tucks away / rarely explains.
- Why it works: Implied hidden knowledge creates a gap with a villain.
- Tech: A menu most phones tuck away.
- Fitness: The rest-day rule trainers rarely explain.
- Finance: The fee line banks put last.
- Evidence level: Creator-Praxis (S20)
- Careful: High risk of deflating the loop and of unverifiable accusations. Use only when the payoff is real and true.

### H17 Experiment on the viewer's behalf  [P, schwach]
- Formula: I [did odd thing] for [duration] so you do not have to.
- Why it works: Borrowed risk plus a promised outcome.
- Tech: I used only airplane mode for a week.
- Fitness: I trained 10 minutes a day for a month.
- Finance: I tracked every cent for 30 days.
- Evidence level: Creator-Praxis (S20)
- Careful: Needs a real experiment; for a fictional persona keep it clearly framed as such.

### H18 X versus Y verdict  [P, schwach]
- Formula: [A] vs [B]: which [wins on criterion]?
- Why it works: A verdict question with two sides; the viewer picks one and wants to be right.
- Tech: Wi-Fi or mobile data: which drains more?
- Fitness: Running or walking: which burns more?
- Finance: Rent or buy: where does the money go?
- Evidence level: Creator-Praxis (S20)
- Careful: Only if you can actually give a clear verdict inside the video.

### H19 Never-do confession  [P, schwach]
- Formula: I never [common action], and here is why.
- Why it works: A breach of a common habit plus an unanswered reason.
- Tech: I never charge past a full battery on purpose.
- Fitness: I never skip this one warm-up move.
- Finance: I never keep money in one account.
- Evidence level: Creator-Praxis (S20)
- Careful: Opinion hook: the reason must be defensible and sourced.

### H20 Payoff first, then rewind  [P, schwach]
- Formula: Show the result of [process] in frame 1, then go back to the start.
- Why it works: The viewer sees the prize and stays for the how.
- Tech: Frame 1: a fast phone. Rewind: how it got fast.
- Fitness: Frame 1: the finished plank. Now how.
- Finance: Frame 1: the final balance. Back to day one.
- Evidence level: Vendor-Praxis (S21)
- Careful: Do not show a fake result.

### H21 Guess first  [M, mittel]
- Formula: Guess: [quantity / outcome of familiar thing]?
- Why it works: Trying and failing to answer before seeing the answer improves learning of the answer; asking for a countable guess also gives viewers something to write in comments.
- Tech: Guess: what drains first, screen or signal?
- Fitness: Guess how many steps burn one burger.
- Finance: Guess what 5 euros a day becomes.
- Evidence level: labor-mechanismus + eigene Daten (S8, S27)
- Careful: Lab effect is for learning, with caveats about delay and material. In our own data an open number question went with more comments (not proven causal). The answer must be sourced if it is a number.

### H22 Awe-scale fact  [M, mittel]
- Formula: [Familiar thing] is [N] times [bigger / faster / older] than [comparison].
- Why it works: High-arousal emotions such as awe made articles more shareable; low-arousal ones such as sadness less so.
- Tech: Your photo crosses oceans in a blink.
- Fitness: Your heart beats about [N] times today.
- Finance: A small fee can grow into a huge cost.
- Evidence level: Feld-Daten (NYT-Artikel) + Experimente (S7)
- Careful: Studied on articles and sharing, not on scroll-stop. Numbers need a source on screen.

### H23 Dialogue open  [M, schwach]
- Formula: [Figure A] asks a question; [figure B] answers with the first surprise.
- Why it works: Overhearing a question-and-answer exchange engaged learners more than monologue in lab work; in our own test the same joke as a 25 s dialogue beat the 42 s narration.
- Tech: Kid: Why is the phone hot? Phone: Good question.
- Fitness: Kid: Why am I sore? Muscle: That was me.
- Finance: Kid: Where did my allowance go? Wallet: Ask the snacks.
- Evidence level: eigene Daten (n=1 gegen n=1) + Labor (S27, S16)
- Careful: Our comparison changed length and form at the same time (proof-log: cause not provable from one video). A confirmation test was pending.

### H24 Everyday analogy  [P, schwach]
- Formula: [Complex thing] is just [everyday thing] that [twist].
- Why it works: A concrete picture replaces an abstract idea; fits explainers with simple pictograms.
- Tech: Wi-Fi is a radio station for your phone.
- Fitness: A muscle is a rubber band that learns.
- Finance: Interest is rent on borrowed money.
- Evidence level: Buchpraxis (S24)
- Careful: Check the analogy does not mislead.

### H25 Time-promise  [P, schwach]
- Formula: Understand [concept] in [T] seconds.
- Why it works: Caps the time cost before the viewer asks for it.
- Tech: Understand encryption in 20 seconds.
- Fitness: A full warm-up in 30 seconds.
- Finance: Understand compound interest in 25 seconds.
- Evidence level: Vendor-Praxis + interne Prinzipien (S21, S28)
- Careful: Only promise a length you will keep.

### H26 Say it and show it  [M, schwach]
- Formula: Voice states [claim] while frame 1 shows [the thing itself].
- Why it works: Meta reports hooks delivered through both picture and sound were 1.5x more likely to rank top for purchase intent.
- Tech: Voice: this app is slowest. Screen: it loading.
- Fitness: Voice: watch the knees. Screen: knees caving, arrow.
- Finance: Voice: here is the leak. Screen: money dripping from a pipe.
- Evidence level: Plattform-Zahl (Meta, Anzeigen, aus zweiter Hand) (S3)
- Careful: Ads and purchase intent, relayed by a trade site.

### H27 Shared ignorance  [P, schwach]
- Formula: Turns out I never understood [basic thing].
- Why it works: Admitting a gap builds common ground and promises a quick lesson.
- Tech: Turns out I never knew what RAM does.
- Fitness: Turns out I stretched wrong for years.
- Finance: Turns out I misread interest for years.
- Evidence level: Creator-Praxis (S20)
- Careful: Persona must be consistent with the channel.

### H28 But-therefore opener  [P, schwach]
- Formula: [Expected thing]. But [twist]. Here is the [consequence].
- Why it works: A 'but' creates tension at once; cause links replace a list of events.
- Tech: Your phone is fast. But this app is slow.
- Fitness: You train hard. But the scale does not move.
- Finance: You earn more. But you keep the same.
- Evidence level: Autoren-Praxis (Drehbuch) (S23)
- Careful: Craft advice from screenwriters, not a measured effect.

### H29 Text-first hook (sound-off safe)  [M, schwach]
- Formula: Frame 1 already carries the hook as short on-screen text; voice repeats it.
- Why it works: Many people watch with the sound off; TikTok advises text of about 5 to 10 words per second as context.
- Tech: Frame 1 text: Why does your phone get hot?
- Fitness: Frame 1 text: Why are you sore two days later?
- Finance: Frame 1 text: Where does your salary go first?
- Evidence level: Umfrage (Selbstauskunft) + Plattform-Empfehlung (S1, S26)
- Careful: Survey is self-reported; TikTok page gives a recommendation, not a measurement.

### H30 Ranked list teaser  [P, schwach]
- Formula: Top [N] [things] that [outcome].
- Why it works: A list promises a clear end and cheap scanning.
- Tech: Top 3 settings that slow your phone.
- Fitness: Top 3 mistakes in a push-up.
- Finance: Top 3 fees hiding in your account.
- Evidence level: Creator-Praxis (S20)
- Careful: Needs real ranking logic; do not pad.

## Holding techniques

### M01 Re-open a fresh loop at 60-70 percent  [P, schwach]
- Formula: Pay the first question, then open [new question] before the viewer feels done.
- Why it works: Information gaps drive wanting; a viewer who feels complete scrolls. Timing of 60-70 percent is an internal rule, not measured.
- Tech: Paid: that is why it is hot. But when is hot too hot?
- Fitness: Paid: that is why it hurts. But why on day two?
- Finance: Paid: that is the fee. But who sets it?
- Evidence level: interne Prinzipien (Mechanismus: Loewenstein) (S28, S4)
- Careful: Do not use the old Zeigarnik argument (unfinished tasks are remembered better): a 2025 meta-analysis did not support it. Resumption (Ovsiankina) held better.

### M02 Pattern break (Musterbruch)  [M, mittel]
- Formula: Change the established look/sound once at [beat] to mark a turn.
- Why it works: Changes trigger an orienting response and re-focus attention; too many unrelated changes harm memory.
- Tech: Beat 4: the phone icon shatters, the next beat shows it whole.
- Fitness: Beat 4: the person jumps out of the row, bar turns red.
- Finance: Beat 4: the bars flip, the tall one becomes small.
- Evidence level: labor-mechanismus (Mediennutzung) (S14)
- Careful: Lab work on TV material. Use once, tied to the story.

### M03 But / therefore chain  [P, schwach]
- Formula: Link each beat to the last with 'but' or 'therefore', never 'and then'.
- Why it works: Cause links make the viewer predict the next beat; a plain sequence gives no reason to continue.
- Tech: Apps want data. But storage is full. Therefore it slows.
- Fitness: You lift. But you do not rest. Therefore no growth.
- Finance: You save. But prices rise. Therefore it shrinks.
- Evidence level: Autoren-Praxis (Drehbuch) (S23)
- Careful: Not measured.

### M04 Signaling: mark the key word  [M, mittel]
- Formula: Colour or highlight the one [key word] per sentence.
- Why it works: Cues that mark what matters help learners follow multimedia lessons (median effect around d 0.4-0.7 in the multimedia-learning literature, varies by review).
- Tech: Heat slows the CHIP.
- Fitness: Sleep builds the MUSCLE.
- Finance: Fees eat the RETURN.
- Evidence level: labor (Lernen mit Medien) (S13)
- Careful: Effect is on learning, not on retention in a feed. Already part of the motion-explainer look (violet key words).

### M05 Segmenting: one idea per beat  [M, mittel]
- Formula: One idea, one picture, one short sentence per beat.
- Why it works: Learner-paced or chunked lessons beat one continuous block (median d around 0.8 in one review); on edX short videos were far more engaging.
- Tech: Beat: heat. Beat: throttle. Beat: slow phone.
- Fitness: Beat: tear. Beat: repair. Beat: stronger.
- Finance: Beat: deposit. Beat: interest. Beat: more interest.
- Evidence level: labor + Feld (6.9 Mio. edX-Sitzungen) (S13, S15)
- Careful: edX study is on course videos of several minutes, not shorts.

### M06 Coherence: cut what does not serve the promise  [M, mittel]
- Formula: Remove every [line / icon / sound] that does not serve the hook's promise.
- Why it works: Extraneous material hurts learning (median d around 0.9 in recent summary); short form is judged line by line.
- Tech: Cut the history of the chip; keep why it heats.
- Fitness: Cut the anatomy lecture; keep what to do tonight.
- Finance: Cut the bank's history; keep what the fee does.
- Evidence level: labor (Lernen mit Medien) (S13, S28)
- Careful: Effect sizes vary by review and shrink in newer meta-analyses.

### M07 Misconception swap  [M, mittel]
- Formula: Say what the viewer would blame/assume ([wrong cause]), then replace it with [real cause].
- Why it works: A refuted wrong model costs effort and sticks better than a plain explanation.
- Tech: You blame the battery. It is the heat.
- Fitness: You think sweat means fat loss. It is water.
- Finance: You think a raise means richer. Spending rises too.
- Evidence level: video-experiment (Autor-Zusammenfassung) (S9)
- Careful: See H02: learning effect, not shown for retention.

### M08 Progress marker  [M, schwach]
- Formula: Show '[k] of [N]' or a filling bar through [list].
- Why it works: People speed up as they approach a goal; a visible marker makes the end feel close.
- Tech: Reason 2 of 3.
- Fitness: Step 2 of 3.
- Finance: Rule 2 of 3.
- Evidence level: labor/Feld (Prämienprogramme, nicht Video) (S11)
- Careful: Shown for reward programs; transfer to video is our assumption.

### M09 Promise-ahead teaser  [P, schwach]
- Formula: Name the best part before it comes: 'the [ordinal] one is [adjective]'.
- Why it works: A pointer to a coming payoff gives a reason to stay through the next beats.
- Tech: Wait for beat 5. It surprised me.
- Fitness: The last move changes everything.
- Finance: The third fee is the sneaky one.
- Evidence level: Vendor-Praxis (Mikro-Vorschau) (S21)
- Careful: Must pay off exactly as named.

### M10 Escalation ladder  [P, schwach]
- Formula: Three steps of [same thing], each bigger, the last one unexpected.
- Why it works: Rising stakes give a built-in prediction and a punch at the top.
- Tech: 1 tab: fine. 10 tabs: warm. 100 tabs: oven.
- Fitness: 1 rep: easy. 10 reps: burning. 30: shaking.
- Finance: 5 euros a day: nothing. A year: a trip. Ten years: a car.
- Evidence level: eigene Beobachtung (n=1, Ursache nicht beweisbar) (S27)
- Careful: Our best Instagram result used a three-step ladder, but the proof-log says the cause cannot be isolated from one video. Numbers in the example are not sourced.

### M11 Cut rhythm  [P, schwach]
- Formula: New picture every [2 to 2.6] seconds; change the angle or icon, not only the words.
- Why it works: TikTok advises transitions, stickers and graphics to keep viewers engaged; frequent visual change refreshes attention.
- Tech: Chip, fan, battery.
- Fitness: Knee, hip, back.
- Finance: Coin, bar, arrow.
- Evidence level: Plattform-Empfehlung ohne Zahl (S1, S14)
- Careful: Already in motion-explainer pacing. Unrelated cuts can hurt memory.

### M12 Dialogue turn  [M, schwach]
- Formula: A second voice objects or asks [question] at the turn.
- Why it works: Overheard question-and-answer exchange engaged learners in lab work; our dialogue short beat our narrated one (confounded).
- Tech: Phone: I am tired. User: You are a phone.
- Fitness: Muscle: I am repairing. You: Can I train? Muscle: No.
- Finance: Wallet: Feed me. Salary: Gone by the 15th.
- Evidence level: eigene Daten (n=1) + Labor (S27, S16)
- Careful: Same caveat as H23.

### M13 Concrete swap  [P, schwach]
- Formula: Replace each [abstract phrase] with a [number, name or date].
- Why it works: Specific words can be pictured and believed.
- Tech: 'Large file' becomes 'a four-minute video'.
- Fitness: 'Long run' becomes '40 minutes'.
- Finance: 'A lot of fees' becomes '[N] euros a year'.
- Evidence level: interne Prinzipien + Buchpraxis (S28, S24)
- Careful: Only with sourced or self-counted numbers.

### M14 Pause and predict  [M, mittel]
- Formula: Ask 'what happens next?' one beat before the answer.
- Why it works: A failed guess before the answer improves learning of the answer.
- Tech: Beat 3: what drains first, screen or signal?
- Fitness: Beat 3: which knee caves first?
- Finance: Beat 3: which account earns more?
- Evidence level: labor-mechanismus (S8)
- Careful: Lab effect for learning with caveats; keep the pause under one beat.

## Endings and loops

### E01 Seamless loop  [P, schwach]
- Formula: The last line ends mid-sentence and the first line completes it.
- Why it works: Replays: YouTube counts every start and replay as a view since March 2025; Instagram ranks watch time. That loops raise results is not measured by us.
- Tech: Last: 'That is why your charger feels...' First: '...warm. Why?'
- Fitness: Last: 'That is why you are sore...' First: '...two days later.'
- Finance: Last: 'That is why your money...' First: '...shrinks. How?'
- Evidence level: Plattform-Fakt (Zählweise), Wirkung unbelegt (S18, S19)
- Careful: A loop can feel forced; use only if the sentence still reads naturally.

### E02 Open-number comment question  [M, schwach]
- Formula: Ask for a countable answer: '[Your number]? Write it below.'
- Why it works: Own data: the video with an open number question got 13 comments after about two days against 1-2 for two closed-punchline videos of the same length. Instagram names sends and likes per reach as key signals.
- Tech: How many tabs are open? Write the number.
- Fitness: How many hours do you sleep? Write it.
- Finance: What rate does your account pay? Write it.
- Evidence level: eigene Daten (3 Videos, kein Beweis) (S27, S19)
- Careful: Proof-log calls it a hint, not proof; a confirmation video was pending.

### E03 Send-worthy end  [M, schwach]
- Formula: Send this to [specific person type] who [habit].
- Why it works: Instagram's head named sends among top ranking signals and, in another answer, sends per reach as the strongest correlate of reach (secondhand report).
- Tech: Send this to the friend with 100 tabs.
- Fitness: Send this to your gym partner.
- Finance: Send this to someone who loves subscriptions.
- Evidence level: Plattform-Angabe (aus zweiter Hand) (S19)
- Careful: Statement is about ranking, not about how to write the line. Use sparingly with the channel's one CTA rule.

### E04 Next-part tease  [M, schwach]
- Formula: End on the open question of the next video: 'Part 2: [question]'.
- Why it works: In written-story experiments, cliffhanger endings raised the wish to read on, without raising enjoyment.
- Tech: Part 2: what happens when it freezes.
- Fitness: Tomorrow: why day two hurts most.
- Finance: Next: the fee nobody reads.
- Evidence level: Experiment (Textgeschichten) (S17)
- Careful: Written stories, not short video. Only if Part 2 really comes.

### E05 Peak-end: finish on the strongest beat  [M, mittel]
- Formula: Put the most surprising or useful beat last, not a recap.
- Why it works: People rate episodes by their peak and their end and neglect duration.
- Tech: Last beat: the one-tap fix, big and alone.
- Fitness: Last beat: the 10-second move that saves the knee.
- Finance: Last beat: the fee, then what it cost.
- Evidence level: Labor (Erlebnisbewertung) (S12)
- Careful: Lab episodes (films, pain), not feed videos.

### E06 Callback to the hook image  [P, schwach]
- Formula: The last frame changes the hook's picture: [open thing] resolved.
- Why it works: Closes the first loop visibly and rewards the whole watch.
- Tech: Chip glowed in frame 1; last frame it cools.
- Fitness: Torn fibre in frame 1; healed at the end.
- Finance: Shrinking coin in frame 1; growing coin at the end.
- Evidence level: Vendor-Praxis (S21)
- Careful: Do not leave a different loop open at the same time.

### E07 One single action  [M, schwach]
- Formula: One verb, one action: [Follow / comment / send] for [one benefit].
- Why it works: Own data: after adding a comment call, the first test video got 3 comments on YouTube in about 2 h versus almost none before. TikTok advises a clear call to action.
- Tech: Follow for one phone fix a day.
- Fitness: Follow for one move a day.
- Finance: Follow for one money rule a day.
- Evidence level: eigene Daten (1 Video) + Plattform-Empfehlung (S27, S1)
- Careful: One video, other changes possible. Never stack two asks.

### E08 One-line takeaway  [P, schwach]
- Formula: Close with [one repeatable line] the viewer can say back.
- Why it works: A simple, memorable summary is easier to repeat and share.
- Tech: Heat in, speed out.
- Fitness: Rest is part of training.
- Finance: Small fees, big loss.
- Evidence level: Buchpraxis (S24)
- Careful: Not measured.
